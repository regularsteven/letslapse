# Shape detection: review of the port, and the next method

Written 2026-09-12, the same day as `handover-2026-09-12.md`, by the agent that picked that
handover up. Two jobs, as the handover framed them: audit what was built (its §5 checklist and
whatever else fell out), and look for a better detector, slower allowed. Everything below was
measured on this Mac against the same 153 labels over 37 pictures; every number is a run block in
`tools/shapebench/work/results/` and can be regenerated with the commands in §7.

## 0. The answers first

1. **The port is sound.** The Moore tracer produces exactly OpenCV's outer borders on 13,443
   components of the Kit's own maps (Jaccard 1.0, every one) and on every synthetic edge case the
   handover asked about; the polygon IoU, the ellipse fit and the polygon simplification agree
   with OpenCV to rounding; the maps themselves agree pixel-for-pixel to 99.0–99.98 %. One latent
   bug: the tracer's step limit of 16·(w + h) cuts a long thin border short (demonstrated on a
   serpentine; the longest real contour on this corpus came within 11 % of the limit). Fixed, with
   a test.
2. **The rig was under-counting the Kit by five hits.** Its consensus dedupe after full-resolution
   refinement merged pairs of Kit shapes 5–10 % apart (a tile's outer and inner edge, both
   labelled). The vision path now scores the Kit's shapes as the Kit emits them. A rounding in
   `serialise_shape` could also write an orientation of 180.0, which the schema refuses; fixed.
3. **The gates are not overfitted to the tuning pictures.** On the 12 pictures labelled after the
   map choices were made, the Kit's recall is 0.51 against 0.45 on the 25 it was tuned on.
4. **Handover direction 1 is worth nothing here.** Full-resolution ROI re-tracing buys the
   reference 0 hits (and costs 7 false positives); a third proposal scale at full resolution buys
   1 hit for 6 false positives and 60 % more time. Both are measured, not argued.
5. **Edge drawing is the next method.** Ported to the rig as the `edge-drawing` detector
   (`tools/shapebench/detect_ed.py`, OpenCV's ximgproc implementation): on its own 74 hits /
   46 false positives against the Kit's 74 / 69 (scored as it emits, §1); its edge chains alone
   51 / 9; with its ellipse hypotheses gated on native edge support 66 / 23. Its false positives
   are mostly nest members nobody drew: precision excluding those is 87 % against the Kit's 68 %.
   **In union with the Kit: 97 of 153 labels (recall 0.48 → 0.63).** It runs in 130 ms at full
   resolution.
6. **What nobody finds is rectangles.** 48 labels are missed by the Kit, the reference and edge
   drawing together; 40 are rectangles or squares, 31 of them small (cobbles, tiles, panes,
   plates on brackets). Rectangle grouping from line segments is the open problem (§5).

## 2. Where the misses are, from the candidate logs

A script joined every missed label with the nearest candidate row of the run (bbox IoU ≥ 0.5)
and classified it. Reference (`a49d2b2eb28e`, 87 misses): **70 proposed and refused** — the
region exists, its traced boundary does not fit: "N vertices, not 4" with N = 5–17 and an ellipse
IoU of 0.50–0.89 against the 0.90 gate, in that order of frequency; 16 never proposed by any map
at either scale; 1 deduped. Kit (`63726d7e9a88`, 84 misses): 60 absent from its output, 29 of
them with a Kit refusal of the right size at the right place among the 24 it keeps (the same
vertex and IoU refusals, plus "outline off the edges: support 0.48/0.49 < 0.50" eight times and
two Hough rims at support 0.30–0.33); 9 lost to the rig's dedupe (§1); 9 accepted with the wrong
geometry, all nest members whose sibling matched; 6 refused by the shared pass.

So the misses are not missing proposals and not gate thresholds (the handover's offline sweep
already showed no gate is worth relaxing): they are contaminated boundaries — a plate merged
with its bracket, a frame with its mullion, a rim with the ornament next to it — which is what a
closing-based map does by construction. A detector that produces one-pixel, connected boundaries
without a closing step is the thing to try, and that is direction 2.

## 4. Edge drawing

Edge Drawing (Topal & Akinlar 2012; EDPF for the parameter-free validation; EDLines and EDCircles
on top) extracts anchors on the gradient ridge and links them into one-pixel, ordered, connected
chains by "smart routing" along the gradient direction. There is no threshold map, no closing
size, no morphological step — a plate's outline is one chain and the bracket under it another,
which is exactly the failure the Kit's maps have. OpenCV ships it in `cv2.ximgproc` (contrib),
which the rig's venv lacks: `pip install opencv-contrib-python-headless` in place of
`opencv-python-headless` (a superset; one `cv2` only) and `shapebench.py ed` runs.

What the rig detector does (`tools/shapebench/detect_ed.py`, detector id `edge-drawing`):

- ED in parameter-free mode at 1024, 2048 and native resolution (a scale is never upscaled).
- **Closed chains** — a chain whose two ends meet within 15 % of its box, or any two points far
  apart along a chain that coincide within 3 px (a loop with a tail) — become regions, prefiltered
  like the reference's (extent 0.08–0.98, solidity ≥ 0.6), deduped at bbox IoU 0.8 with native
  scale first. A native-scale chain is its own full-resolution measurement (`refined: true`); a
  lower-scale chain goes through the rig's re-trace with the identity rule (measuring it as traced
  instead changes nothing: 74 / 46 either way).
- **EDCircles' ellipses** (arc grouping with a-contrario validation) are synthetic outlines and are
  treated like Vision's: re-traced against the maps; where nothing confirms one it stands as
  proposed. Optional gates: `requireRefined` (58 / 15) and `minEdgeSupport` — the share of the
  hypothesis outline within 3 px of ED's own native edge map (0.5: 70 / 39 · 0.6: 68 / 36 ·
  **0.7: 66 / 23** · 0.8: 63 / 15).
- Then the shared §3 fitting pass and the consensus dedupe at 0.9, unchanged.

Attribution of the default run's 74 hits: native chains 33 (7 FP), lower-scale chains 7 (1 FP),
confirmed ellipse hypotheses 18 (7 FP), unconfirmed ellipse hypotheses 16 (**31 FP**). The chains
carry the small shapes (33 of their 40 hits are small); the arc grouping carries the rims the
tracer cannot close (the ribbed medallions and rose windows of the handover's §6) and, ungated,
the phantoms (an arch's crown, a cobble ring). Runtime: ED itself 130 ms at 12 MP, 30 ms at 2048;
the rig's measurement dominates the 1.1 s per picture.

Per band and primitive (hits / labels), the Kit as it emits (`db067013f4a9`), edge drawing
(`7c6eba8d96e1`) and edge drawing with the gated ellipses (`4da2cec76f3e`):

| | ellipse (56) | rectangle (97) | small (111) | medium (31) | large (11) | FP nested in a label | precision excl. nested |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kit | 28 | 46 | 52 | 15 | 7 | 34 of 69 | 68 % |
| edge drawing | **38** | 36 | **56** | 12 | 6 | 35 of 46 | **87 %** |
| edge drawing, gated ellipses | 30 | 36 | 52 | 8 | 6 | 20 of 23 | 96 % |

Edge drawing is the better ellipse finder (38 against 28 of 56) and the Kit the better rectangle
finder (46 against 36 of 97); the union takes both. With the ellipse gate at 0.7 the attribution
becomes: chains 42 hits / 8 FP, confirmed hypotheses 16 / 4, unconfirmed 8 / 11.

**Port cost and plan.** ED with EDPF validation is about 600–800 lines of Swift (gradient,
anchors, routing, segment NFA); EDCircles is another ~1,500 (arc extraction, joining, ellipse
fitting, NFA). The chains alone are worth porting first: they are the precise half, they are
what finds the small shapes, and they slot into the file pass as a fourth candidate source
measured by the existing `RegionProposals` fits — the union with the current Kit is the number to
expect. The arc grouping second, with the native edge-support gate at 0.7, not before. Neither
belongs in the viewfinder profile. OpenCV's `edge_drawing.cpp` (BSD) is the reference to port
from; the rig scores the port the way it scored everything else.

## 5. The hard set

48 labels are found by nothing (Kit ∪ edge drawing ∪ reference): 17 small rectangles, 14 small
squares, 7 medium rectangles, 1 medium and 1 large square, 3 small circles, 3 small ellipses,
2 medium circles. By picture: the cobble ring DD08E911 (8), the tiled wall 606B29B0 (7), B46433F9
(5), 96752BAC and 947A57FC (4 each). The list is in `hard-set.txt` beside this file's scratch
data if regenerated (`union_score.py`), and it is where the next investigation starts.

Rectangles from line segments (direction 3) were tried in two forms. The first, facing parallel
pairs whose two closing sides only had to lie on edge pixels, drowned in texture: 16 hits for
622 false positives on five pictures, 112 accepted shapes per picture. The second form required each closing side to be covered ≥ 60 % by real EDLines segments within 3 px and 8°, after merging collinear pieces: at 1024 px on the same five pictures it gains nothing (12 hits with or without it, 55 against 3 false positives), and over the corpus it buys 9 hits for 249 false positives (70 / 281 against 61 / 32). Both forms live behind `quads.use` in `detect_ed.py`, off by default, so the next attempt starts from a measured baseline rather than from zero. (Its collinear merge had an infinite loop in the first cut — a segment already emitted in a pass could be merged again by a later one — which is why the full-resolution runs never finished; fixed before these numbers were taken.) The lesson
is the same as EDCircles': a hypothesis needs a validation that texture cannot pass — for
rectangles, four real segments meeting at four real junctions, not edge pixels along a line.

- **Preview, Use all and the score sheet (later that evening, Steven's follow-up).** Every
  candidate of a find is drawn over the picture (`FoundShapesOverlay`: dashed while pending,
  solid once added, bold with a halo when hovered, and the hover runs both ways between the
  list and the picture). The detector menus gained **Use all**: every engine of the roster runs
  in turn and the shapes are merged first (same kind, bounds-IoU ≥ 0.8, `ShapeFinder.merge`),
  so one object found by three engines is one row with "3 of 7: Std, Std+chains, PyED" under
  it. `ShapeDetectorFeedback` (`ShapeDetectorFeedback.ndjson` in the library root, append-only)
  records every engine run, every proposal with how many engines agreed, and the person's
  verdicts: Add is a hit for every proposer and a miss for every engine that ran without
  proposing it; a candidate left un-added when the find is cleared or replaced is a false
  alarm for every proposer; Undo turns an Add into a rejection; a shape the register already
  held is a hit for whoever found it again. Because merging comes first, an engine is never
  marked wrong for a shape another engine's copy got accepted — that is the answer to the
  "two detectors, one shape" question. The Find shapes sheet's **Detector report** card sums
  the sheet per engine (runs · found · agreed · hits · passed over · missed · median ms); a
  verdict is a label with a picture, so the sheet is also the seed of a future label set.
- **Hover and rejection, after Steven's hands-on test.** The first overlay let the last outline
  take every hover: on macOS `.onHover` tracks a view's frame, not its `contentShape`, and each
  outline was a picture-sized view. Now the viewer computes the hover from the mouse over the
  whole picture (`onContinuousHover` → `FoundShapesOverlay.hoverTarget`: the nearest outline
  within 10 pt, else the current candidate while the mouse is on its pill) and the overlay is
  drawing only, except the pill. Every candidate has a rejection path: a red cross in its row
  and in the pill, "Rejected · undo" after (the line leaves the picture), Reject all beside Add
  all; undo withdraws a verdict (`pending` on the score sheet). Verified with real mouse input:
  hover from the picture lights the row and shows the pill, the pill's tick adds (solid line,
  register written), the row's cross rejects (line gone, `rejected` recorded). Second round from
  Steven's test: a pill above the bounding box was out of reach — the mouse had to leave the
  circle, and the small rectangle inside it took the hover on the way. The pill now appears
  where the mouse touched the outline, and a hover from the picture is sticky: it ends only when
  another outline is hovered, the find changes, or the shape is rejected (`foundHoverAnchor`,
  keyed by candidate; `hoverTarget` checks the pill first, then the nearest outline, then keeps
  the current one).

So the port buys 8 hits for 14 false positives at three scales (10 gained, 2 lost) — real, but
half of what the prototype's chains promised and none of what its ellipse hypotheses (EDCircles,
34 hits on their own) would add. The port job's diagnosis, in its own measurements:

- **The port is the algorithm.** On the Kit's own gray plane (its dump, its 5-tap blur) the
  Swift chains are byte-identical to `cv2.ximgproc`'s at 1024 and 2048 (732 of 732 and 1,589 of
  1,589 chains, every edge pixel shared, identical loop counts). Two details came from OpenCV
  5's source: RIGHT/DOWN walks test the C diagonal for an existing edge before A (LEFT/UP the
  reverse), and a forward path drops its first pixel before the join cleanup.
- **The prototype's 51 / 9 was never the right yardstick for the Kit.** Run in the Kit's exact
  configuration (chains only, fixed thresholds, 1024 + 2048, measured as traced, no Vision
  passes) the prototype scores 50 / 11 — parameter-free mode, rig refinement and the native
  scale each move it by one or two. The chains' marginal gain in the Kit over the Vision passes
  alone is +21 hits / +26 FP at two scales, +27 / +22 at three.
- **Every added false positive is a nest member or a duplicate.** At three scales the "other"
  false positives stay at the default's 31; the +14 are nested members nobody drew (+14 nested)
  and duplicates of a shape the Vision passes already had at bounds-IoU under the 0.9 merge bar
  (B848CA9E's two are exactly this). A prototype-style 0.8 merge among the chains themselves
  would remove 2 FP and 1 TP, so cross-scale copies are not it. The rest is gate noise: the rig's
  planes (ImageIO decode, Core Graphics downscale) and the Kit's (`cv2.INTER_AREA`) differ enough
  that loops on DD08E911 land either side of "5 vertices", 8.5° against 8° and fill 0.848 against
  0.85 in both directions.
- Chain candidates outrank region candidates at the merge (regions kept 87 → 72, chains kept
  43), with no label lost to it.

Cost, measured again on a quiet machine: +100–135 ms per 12 MP picture at 1024 + 2048, +560 ms
with the native scale (a 12 MP decode plus ED over it). The app's "Standard + edge chains" mode
runs 1024 + 2048; the native scale is one flag away (`edgeChainLongEdges`). Not ported, by the
plan: the NFA validation and EDCircles — the ellipse hypotheses are the next 20-odd hits, with the
0.7 native edge-support gate the prototype measured. The port job also extracted the region
pass's fit-and-tie rule into `RegionProposals.fit` so both passes judge outlines by one rule
(byte-identical output on the default profile), and left a `LAPSE_EDGES_DEBUG=<dir>` dump hook
(`gray-<L>.pgm`, `chains-<L>.json`) beside `LAPSE_REGIONS_DEBUG`.
