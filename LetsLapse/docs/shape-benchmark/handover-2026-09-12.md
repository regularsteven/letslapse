# Shape detection: handover for review, audit and the next method

Written 2026-09-12 for whoever picks this up next — a reviewer auditing what was built, and an
investigator looking for a better detector, slower allowed. It is self-contained: read this,
then the code and data it points at. Nothing here needs the conversation that produced it.

Branch `ios-app`, commits `195223e` → `408737d` → `91ace1b` → `880b2d5` → `c3f2224` (in that
order; `git log --stat` on each is the change record). The brief that started it is
`docs/shape-benchmark/brief.md`; the findings as they were written up while happening are
`docs/shape-benchmark/report.md`; the generated tables and 37 review pictures are beside it.

## 1. The problem

LetsLapse's Photo mode finds circles, ovals, squares and rectangles in still photographs so that
a sequence of photos can be aligned on one shape (Shape-mation: the sign holds still while the
world changes behind it). The shipped detector ("Find shapes", the file pass of
`ShapeDetector`) found 19 % of the shapes a person marks. The task was to measure that
honestly, decide whether an AI ranking layer was needed (it was not), and raise recall.

What a "shape" is, for this problem — the brief's §3 rules, applied everywhere:

- **Rectangle:** four straight sides; every corner within 8° of 90° *in image space* (so an
  oblique, keystoned plate is refused by design, even though the app can rectify one from the
  lens); opposite sides within 10 %; the region fills at least 85 % of its minimum-area
  rectangle. Square when the aspect is within 5 % of 1.
- **Ellipse:** a direct least-squares fit whose area IoU with the traced region is at least
  0.90; axis ratio at least 0.40. Circle when the ratio is at least 0.95.
- **Size floor:** the shape's extent (bounding box over the frame's matching side) at least
  0.10. Bands: small < 0.35, medium < 0.65, large.
- **Two detections are the same shape** (the consensus rule, used to match a detection to a
  label): same primitive, centre offset ≤ 2 % of the diagonal, mask IoU ≥ 0.70 at a 1024-px
  working scale, aspect within 10 %. Matching is greedy one-to-one by IoU.

Policy decisions made by the product owner during the work, now load-bearing:

- **Flat nests.** A sign's rim and its disc, a tyre and its rim and its hubcap, a frame and
  its pane are all shapes; every member the passes can measure goes in the register.
  Shape-mation takes the biggest per photo (its builder already sorted largest-first). So a
  detector must not merge concentric members: "the same shape" inside a detector is
  near-identical only (bounds IoU ≥ 0.9).
- **File-pass floor 0.10 of the short edge**, no pixel minimum (SIZE = All was 1/6 + 400 px;
  the viewfinder keeps 1/6 — at 384 px a 0.10 shape is 38 px).
- **No AI ranking layer** (brief §8): the rules plus the floor accept a median of 2–3 shapes per
  picture; the problem was never choosing among hundreds.

### 2.2 The detector — `Kit/Sources/LetsLapseKit/Shapes/`

`ShapeDetector.detectWithDiagnostics` (668 lines), on a 1024-px long-edge copy of the picture:

1. **Quads** — `VNDetectRectanglesRequest` (≤ 12, confidence ≥ 0.6, min size 0.08), then an
   edge-support gate (share of the perimeter with a CIEdges edge under it ≥ 0.45 — Vision
   reports rectangles over blank sky, and a circle's silhouette as a rounded square).
2. **Ellipses** — `VNDetectContoursRequest` at 512 px over 8 passes (contrast 1/2/3 × dark/light
   polarity, plus two CIEdges maps), each contour: ≥ 24 points, polygon approximation > 6
   vertices, Halir–Flusser fit, residual ≤ 0.04, angular coverage ≥ 0.70, obliquity ≥ 0.25,
   size gates. Vision's tracer is superlinear in resolution and serialised per process, which is
   why 512.
3. **Rims** — `EdgeCircleDetector` (370 lines): a Hough circle vote on edge points at 512 px with
   support / coverage / gap / wide-hole gates, for rims the tracer cannot close (ribbed medallions,
   clock faces). Off-centre near-copies of a kept rim are "lobe votes" and merged.
4. **Region proposals** — `RegionProposals` (804 lines, new): the reference's maps, ported. On the
   8-bit luma: 5-tap Gaussian; Canny with high = Otsu split of the picture (floor 40), low = ½
   high, L2 gradient, OpenCV's NMS sectors and hysteresis; adaptive Gaussian-mean threshold
   (51 taps, σ 8, C 5) in both polarities; each raw map closed at 5, 9 and 15 px with disc
   kernels (vImage dilate; vImage erode does not honour a 0/255 kernel, so erode = complement ∘
   dilate ∘ complement); each closed map also as its "enclosed interiors" (flood the background
   from the frame edge, keep what it cannot reach). That is 18 maps per scale, run at 1024 and
   2048 (a downscale of the input to each). Contours: white 8-connected components' outer
   borders and black 4-connected holes not touching the frame, traced Moore-neighbour style
   from each component's first pixel in scan order. Per contour: ≥ 40 points, extent 0.08–0.98,
   solidity ≥ 0.60; regions deduped at bbox IoU 0.80, larger first; **outline edge support**
   ≥ 0.5 (share of the traced points within 3 px of a Canny edge — the adaptive threshold turns
   any large flat region into a blob whose boundary sits ~2σ inside the real edge, and that blob
   fits a perfect circle of the wrong size); then both §3 fits: minimum-area rectangle by
   rotating calipers over the hull, `approxPolyDP`-faithful simplification at 2 % of the
   perimeter (two-point seed, recursive split, then drop any vertex within ε of its neighbours'
   chord — without the last step a seed on a straight side is a fifth corner), ellipse by
   `EllipseFit` with area IoU via Sutherland–Hodgman against a 72-gon of the fit. The better
   score wins (rectangle wins a tie within 0.02 when its sides are straight). Candidates from
   both scales are unioned, scored best-first, size-gated, and admitted where no kept shape
   overlaps at bounds IoU > 0.9.
5. **Merging** — `sameShapeIoU` 0.9 everywhere a shape is compared to a shape inside the
   detector (was bounds 0.5, which merged nest members and kept the higher-scoring one, not the
   outer). A quad over an ellipse's bounds at ≥ 0.6 is still dropped (Vision reading a
   silhouette as a rounded square).

Profiles (`ShapeSearch`): `fileSettings()` is what Find shapes and the capture-time register run
— region pass on at [1024, 2048], All floor 0.10 of the short edge, no pixel minimum;
`liveSettings()` is the viewfinder — 384 px, one or two contour passes, no region pass, floor
1/6. The sensitivity dial moves contrast passes, residual and support gates; measured on this
corpus it moves recall by ±3 points and precision by ±20, and is not where the recall was.

Diagnostics: every pass counts what it offered and refused (`quadsOffered`, `ellipseFits`,
`rimPeaks`, `regionMaps`, `regions`, `regionsKept`), the nearest 24 refusals with a reason and a
margin. `lapse shapes <image> --verbose` prints them; `--json` is what the rig consumes.
Registers on devices carry these diagnostics, so `Diagnostics.init(from:)` decodes older files.

The app: `App/Shapemation/ShapeFinder.swift` runs the file profile over every photo project whose
register is missing or from an older `detectorVersion` (now 2), keeps hand-drawn shapes as drawn,
snaps captured viewfinder shapes to their file fit at bounds IoU ≥ 0.7 (identity kept), carries
the viewfinder trail over, writes `shapes.json`. The Shape-mation builder picks the largest
admissible shape per photo.

### 2.3 The ground truth

37 photographs from the "Shape testing" projects in the owner's library (Prague streets and
facades, signs, wheels, manholes, doorbells, rose windows; 33 JPEG, 4 DNG, all 12 MP, shot *at*
a target). Labelled by the owner alone in two sittings on 2026-09-11/12 with `label.html`:
rectangles as four clicked corners (corners placed where the straight sides would meet, not on
the rounding), ellipses as 8–12 rim points fitted by least squares, every nest member drawn in
the second sitting, older shapes redrawn where loose. **105 shapes; 153 above the floor over the
37 pictures; 111 small, 31 medium, 11 large; 31 circles, 20 ellipses, 22 squares, 32 rectangles.**
Nothing the labeller saw came from a detector. Caveats an auditor should weigh: one labeller,
one sitting per pass, no inter-rater measurement; nests were completed against a list of where
the detector had found unlabelled members (so the completion pass was not blind); the
"other edge of a border" question (a frame's inner edge against its outer) was left to the
labeller's judgement per picture and is not consistent.

## 3. The numbers

All against the 153 labels over 37 pictures, flat merging, consensus-rule matching. "Excl.
nested" leaves out false positives whose bounding box lies inside a labelled shape's — under
flat those are members nobody drew, not detector errors, but they are counted and shown.

| detector | hits | FP | nested | precision strict / excl. nested | recall | small / medium / large |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kit as shipped before this work (Vision passes, floor 1/6) | 26 | 30 | 18 | 46 % / 68 % | 17 % | 14/111 · 10/31 · 2/11 |
| Python reference (`detect_opencv.py`) | 66 | 43 | 24 | 61 % / 78 % | 43 % | 43/111 · 16/31 · 7/11 |
| **Kit now, `lapse shapes` on the files** | **72** | 66 | 31 | 52 % / 68 % | **47 %** | 50/111 · 15/31 · 7/11 |
| Kit now, DNGs decoded as the app decodes them | 69 | 64 | 31 | 52 % / 68 % | 45 % | |
| **In the app: Find shapes, registers scored** | **71** | 72 | 33 | 50 % / 65 % | **46 %** | |
| the registers as they were before (old detector + the shooter's kept shapes) | 25 | 35 | 11 | 42 % / 51 % | 19 % (of 132 on 33 pictures) | |

Geometry where a shape is found is not the problem: aspect error median 1.1 %, centre offset
median 0.10 % of the diagonal, orientation error median 0.3°, subclass agreement ~90 %.
Runtime: 2.8 s per project median in the Mac app (M4 Max, Release), of which the region pass is
~1.2 s; p90 19 s and max 58 s are the Hough rim pass on cobbles and facades and pre-date this
work. Not yet run on a phone.

Reach: every configuration of the Kit together finds 37 % of what was labelled at the 68-label
stage; the union of everything ever tried was 46 of 68 then. **22 of the original 68 labels were
found by nothing**, and after the relabel the never-found set is dominated by small shapes:
of 111 small labels the best single run finds 50.

## 4. What was learned, in the order it would bite a reviewer

### 4.1 Measurement traps that changed numbers

- **Refinement must not change identity.** The rig re-traces a proposal at full resolution and
  replaces it with the best-IoU contour (≥ 0.8). A 0.93-IoU contour of plate-plus-frame replaced
  a Kit quad that was right to 1.5 % and scored it as a miss. `fitting.measure` now keeps the
  proposal's verdict unless the refined shape matches it under the consensus rule. Every
  detector block was re-measured after this; the shipped pass went from 16 % to 19 %.
- **Proposals that survive as their own outline score IoU 1.0 by construction.** A Vision
  ellipse with no matching full-res contour, or a Hough circle "refined" by gathering edge
  points in a band around itself, cannot fail the ellipse gate. Hough proposals and the band
  refinement were built and removed for this reason; `provenance.refined: false` marks the
  shapes measured as proposed, and the report counts them.
- **The consensus rule cannot see nests.** Two rings 8 % apart in radius overlap at 0.85 ≥ 0.70,
  so a rule meant for "is this the labelled shape" also says "these two are one shape". Inside
  detectors it now is not used for dedupe; for matching, greedy one-to-one by IoU pairs the
  closer ring and the other counts as a miss, which is the honest reading.
- **The app decodes DNG at the detector's size straight from raw** (CIRAWFilter, and ImageIO's
  thumbnail in `lapse`); a full render downsized differs by 2.5 grey levels on average and a
  shape at the size floor flipped on it. The rig now hands `lapse` the DNG and renders its own
  frames with ImageIO. The 16-bit Display P3 container the raw decode arrives in was not the
  cause (tested).

### 4.2 Detector traps

- The median-σ auto-Canny (Rosebrock's heuristic) goes blind on a bright wall (median 165 →
  high 220): the enamel plate's border vanishes. Otsu-split thresholds work across this corpus.
- A plate's edge ring breaks at its rounded corners; no single closing size seals it without
  merging neighbours elsewhere (15 px finds the plate, 9 px the medallion). Hence three sizes
  unioned and the enclosed-interiors map.
- Rectangles from contours need `approxPolyDP`'s cleanup pass; a two-point seed alone leaves a
  fifth vertex on a straight side. Found by running OpenCV's `approxPolyDP` on the Kit's own
  dumped maps and getting 4 where the port got 5.
- The Gaussian-mean adaptive threshold makes a 2σ-inset blob of every large flat region. At
  the reference's full-resolution refinement 16 px was noise; at 1024 px in the Kit it is a
  20 % radius error on a small shape and fits a perfect circle. The outline-edge-support gate is
  the answer and is a real addition over the Python.
- vImage's erode does not take a 0/255 structuring kernel (an excluded position saturates to 0
  and wins the minimum); dilate does. Measured on a 12×5 plane.
- `-Onone` test builds run the region pass ~20× slower (50 s per picture at two scales); the
  detector tests run the Vision machine alone (`.visionOnly`) and `RegionProposalsTests`
  cover the region pass on a synthetic card.

### 4.3 Product traps

- Find shapes built `ShapeDetector()` from raw `Settings()` rather than the file profile, and
  decoded at 1024 px; the settings and the picture size now come from one place
  (`Settings.decodeLongEdge`).
- Captured viewfinder shapes kept their 384-px geometry and suppressed the file pass's sharper
  detection of the same object at bounds IoU 0.4 — a threshold written for frame-to-frame drift
  on a hand-held viewfinder. Still-against-still pairing is 0.7 (`stillMatchThreshold`).
- A re-analysis rebuilt the register without its viewfinder trail.
- `detectorVersion` existed on the register and nothing read it; 199 registers in this library
  were at version 1 with no way to redo them. Version 2 now re-offers them.

## 5. Audit checklist (things I would want a second pair of eyes on)

1. **Moore tracer correctness** (`RegionProposals.trace` / `moore`): the "previous pixel"
   backtrack variant with a Jacob's-criterion stop that compares the next step to the first;
   holes traced as black 4-components with an 8-neighbour walk restricted to the label. Verified
   on a synthetic card (disc, rectangle, trapezoid, a light disc inside a dark square) and by
   agreement with OpenCV's `findContours` on dumped maps for one picture — not proven in
   general. A thin diagonal line, a one-pixel bridge between blobs, and a component touching the
   frame are the cases to try.
2. **Sutherland–Hodgman with a non-convex subject** (`polygonIoU`): the clip polygon is the
   convex 72-gon, the subject is the traced contour, which can be self-touching. The area of the
   output is right for simple polygons; a self-intersecting trace could over- or under-count.
   `testPolygonIoU` covers an L-shape only.
3. **Gate calibration on the same corpus that scores it.** Every threshold in `RULES` came from
   the brief, but the enclosed-fill idea, the three closing sizes, the edge-support gate at 0.5,
   the 0.8 refinement bar and the 0.9 merge bar were all chosen while looking at this corpus. The
   offline gate sweep in the report shows none of the §3 gates is worth relaxing here, which is
   evidence the gates are not overfitted; the map choices have no such check. A second corpus is
   the only honest test.
4. **The 2048 scale doubles proposals and false positives** (+9 hits, +12 FP at the 68-label
   stage). Whether that trade is right depends on how the picker feels with 18–26 shapes on a
   facade; the median stays at 3.
5. **`stillMatchThreshold` 0.7 versus nests.** A captured outer rim whose own detection is
   missed can still snap to the inner disc at IoU 0.85. Rare here (12 captured shapes match no
   label either way); the fix would be a size-ratio test alongside the IoU.
6. **DNG fidelity.** `lapse` and the app decode raw at 2048 from the file; the rig's reference
   uses a full ImageIO render. The labels were drawn on a rawpy render (same geometry, different
   tone). Only 4 of 37 assets are DNG; the effect measured was 3 hits.
7. **The rig's `refine_region` picks the best-IoU contour ≥ 0.8 across 18 maps in an ROI at
   full resolution** — a 3024-px picture's ROI can be large and this is the slow part of
   `vision` (4 minutes for 37 register blocks). It is also where a measurement can drift to the
   "same object, other edge"; the identity rule catches the aspect/centre cases, not a pure
   radius change on a circle within IoU 0.7.
8. **Label noise.** One labeller; the nest-completion pass was guided. A blind relabel of 10
   pictures by someone else would price the noise; differences under ~8 points between runs are
   inside the intervals anyway.

## 6. Where the misses are, concretely

From the review overlays (`docs/shape-benchmark/review/`, green = label, cyan = matched, orange =
false positive, red = miss) and the candidate logs:

- **Small shapes** (extent 0.10–0.20): 61 of 111 small labels are missed by the best run. At
  1024 px a 0.10 shape is 77 px; at 2048, 154 px. Contours at that size carry pixel stairs and
  text, the fill gate and the four-corner test are brittle, and the Vision passes at 512 px see
  38 px. The second scale bought 9; a third (full resolution, ROI-only) is the obvious next lever.
- **Ornate and ribbed rims** — rose windows, the oval window in a carved surround, the sunburst
  medallion at a distance, the radiator valve: traced contours reach ellipse IoU 0.50–0.86
  against the 0.90 gate. The Hough rim pass refuses two labelled rings by support 0.33–0.34
  against 0.35 and coverage 0.53 against 0.55.
- **Small dark discs and buttons** (doorbell lenses, bell buttons): low contrast, small, often
  never proposed by any map at any scale.
- **Inner panes behind frames**: proposed but merged with mullions or refused for extra
  vertices.
- **False positives**: facade windows nobody labelled (valid rectangles, not targets), nest
  members nobody drew, and a few genuine phantoms (an arch's top read as a circle; one circle on
  a street scene).

## 7. Directions for a better method (slower is allowed)

In rough order of expected return per effort, with what each would have to beat: 72 hits /
66 false positives on these 153 labels, 2.8 s per picture on an M4 Max.

1. **A third proposal scale at full resolution, per region of interest.** Take every region the
   1024/2048 passes *refuse* for fit (not for size), re-trace it in its own ROI at 3024 px with
   the same maps, refit. That is the reference's `refine_region` moved into the Kit, and the
   reference's edge over the Kit on medium shapes is largely this. Cost: proportional to refused
   regions, bounded by ROI area; ~1–3 s more per picture.
2. **Edge-drawing segment detectors instead of Canny + tracing.** ED / EDPF (parameter-free edge
   drawing), EDLines and EDCircles (Akinlar & Topal) produce clean, one-pixel, *connected* edge
   chains and detect circles and ellipses by arc grouping with an a-contrario false-alarm control
   — exactly the ribbed-rim and broken-ring cases, without a closing size to choose. There is no
   Apple framework for it; it is a port (a few thousand lines of C++ exist under permissive
   licences). Likely the best precision-per-second candidate.
3. **Line-segment grouping for rectangles.** LSD (or EDLines) → segments → junction and
   parallelism grouping into quadrilaterals with the §3 tests on the result. Rectangles are the
   larger class of misses among small shapes and the contour path loses them to merged text and
   mullions; segments do not care what is inside the frame.
4. **Sub-pixel edge refinement of the accepted fits** (Devernay-style, or a 1-D fit across the
   gradient along the shape's normal at each sample). Does not change recall; tightens geometry
   from ~1 % to a fraction of that, which is what alignment quality is made of.
5. **Learned edges or masks as a proposal source, then the same §3 fits.** HED/DexiNed-class
   edge nets give ribbed rims a single clean contour; a promptable segmenter (SAM-family, or its
   fast variants, exported to Core ML) proposes object masks that the §3 pass can fit. Both are
   slow (hundreds of ms to seconds per picture on a phone), both change the app's dependency
   posture, and the measurement question stays the same: the mask is a *proposal*; the fit and
   the gates decide. The rig scores any of these as another run block without change.
6. **A learned scorer is not the next step.** The §8 gate holds — median 3 accepted shapes per
   picture — and precision is lost to unlabelled windows and nest members, which are a labelling
   policy, not a ranking problem.

Whatever is tried: implement it as another `detectorId` (or a `--flags` profile of the Kit),
run `metrics --all-runs`, and let the 153 labels decide. The candidate logs will say *why* each
label was missed before any code is written — that is how every finding above was made.
