# DNG archive conversion — spike report

**Brief:** `docs/dng-archive-spike-brief.md`. **Tool:** `tools/dng-spike`
(`dngspike`, see its README). **Run:** 2026-09-05 on the M4 Max (macOS
15.6.1, Xcode 26.1.1, libjxl 0.12.0, LibRaw 0.22.1); iPhone 16 Pro (iOS 26.6)
and iPad Air 13" M3 (iPadOS 26.6.1) for the device probes. **Numbers:** `matrix.csv` (every conversion),
`matrix.md` (the tables, generated by
`tools/dng-spike/scripts/report-tables.py matrix.csv`), `probe-*.txt/json`
(capability tables). Regenerate with `dngspike bench --set both` (both sets
mounted, ~15 min) and `dngspike bench --throughput 24 --inflight 3 --only …`.

## 0. The answer in one paragraph

Camera-native lossy JPEG XL DNGs at Adobe's size, made on-device without
Adobe, are feasible today: the Kit's own parse of a LetsLapse Bayer DNG (or
LibRaw's unpack of an ARW), a Metal demosaic, a Lanczos resample, a cubic or
gamma stored-value curve and libjxl at distance 0.5–1.0 produce **0.6–1.3 MB
per 8–10 MP frame** in **0.5–1 s on the M4 Max** (libjxl is about half of
it), render within a few percent of the source through Apple's decoder
*directly* — no repack — and round-trip through Adobe DNG Converter. The
mosaic can also be kept: lossless JPEG XL on the Bayer plane beats the
Kit's lossless JPEG by 5–9% and lossy JPEG XL on the mosaic (with a
MapPolynomial, black 0) gives 0.7–1.3 MB camera-native files that Apple and
Adobe both read. There is **no JPEG XL encoder in any Apple framework** —
not on the Mac, not on iOS 26.6 on an A18 Pro or an M3 iPad (the ProRAW JXL
path is not exposed) — so libjxl (BSD-3) is the encoder, on the CPU. Apple's DNG reader
has several level/curve quirks (§4) that decide the exact container shape;
all of them have a working combination.

### 2.3 Stage timings (typical single frame, M4 Max)

| stage | Set 1, LibRaw → Metal → JPEG XL d0.5 e7 10 MP | Set 2, Kit parse → Metal → JPEG XL d1.0 e5 8 MP |
|---|---:|---:|
| decode (LibRaw open + unpack 154 ms + copy / Kit parse + 192 LJ92 tiles) | 165–230 ms | 20–30 ms |
| Metal upload + demosaic + Lanczos + readback | 55–95 ms | 65–80 ms |
| curve (vDSP, 30 M / 24 M samples) | 105–185 ms | 120–130 ms |
| libjxl, 512-px tiles in parallel | 390–930 ms | 235–250 ms |
| container write | 6–11 ms | 7 ms |
| **total** | **0.7–1.4 s** | **0.46 s** |

The Metal step is not GPU-bound: 10–17 ms of demosaic and 13–17 ms of
Lanczos, the rest is the Float32 readback and conversion. The curve step is
`vvpowf` over the whole frame and would move to the GPU for free. libjxl is
the cost; its effort setting is the lever (e3 → 100–230 ms at 8–10 MP, e5
≈ e7 quality at 60% of the time on Set 2). Apple's own decode of a frame
(`CIRAWFilter`, full size) is 300–500 ms after the first call and 1.2 s
cold, so on the Apple-decode route the converter, not the codec, sets the
floor.

Set 1 was shot at one frame every ~3.6 s (0.28 fps): every pipeline above is
faster than the shoot by 7–80×. Peak footprints are the whole process with
three frames in flight; the LinearRaw pipelines carry Float32 intermediates
(RGBA readback 160 MB + RGB 120 MB + stored 60 MB per 10 MP frame), which is
where an iPhone budget of ~150 MB per frame would be met — Float16/UInt16
intermediates or tile-streaming the demosaic, both straightforward. The
`peak_mb` column of the per-frame rows is polluted by the verifier's own
decodes and should be read from the throughput rows only. Thermal state
stayed `nominal` throughout on the Mac. (*the first Set 1 throughput row
inherited the footprint of the Apple-decode row before it.)

### 2.5 On the iPhone 16 Pro (A18 Pro, iOS 26.6, optimised Debug build)

Measured through the app's `LL_DNGARCHIVE` hook on a 12 MP Bayer interval
project of 11 frames (`duplicateAsDNGArchive`, the shipped code path, libjxl
from the static xcframework, two frames in flight unless stated):

| run | per frame (wall) | decode | Metal | curve | libjxl e5 d0.5 | frames/s | out |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 MP, one in flight | **0.50 s** | 62 ms | 63 ms | 85 ms | **280 ms** | 1.9 | 1.5–1.8 MB |
| 8 MP, two in flight | 0.7–0.9 s each | 90–130 ms | 75–90 ms | 120–140 ms | 370–560 ms | **2.1** | same |
| 12 MP (source size), two in flight | 0.9–1.2 s each | 85–130 ms | 75–90 ms | 190–220 ms | 520–770 ms | 1.6 | 2.0–2.5 MB |

The phone converts an 8 MP frame in half a second — libjxl on the A18 Pro's
six cores is within 20% of the M4 Max (280 vs 235–250 ms) because the tiles
already saturate both; a second frame in flight buys only 10%. Set 1's
cadence of one frame every 3.6 s is beaten seven times over; a 486-frame
shoot archives in about four minutes on the phone. The unoptimised Debug
build is 4–6 s per frame — 2 s of it the Kit's lossless-JPEG decode running
without `-O` — so device timings must come from an optimised build.

frames were 1.3 MB, these 3.7). Apple's own DNGs code each Bayer tile as a
two-component lossless JPEG of half the width; the Kit's native decoder
reads that layout since this run (it refused it the first time).

## 3. Quality

Method, as in `LossyLinearDNGTests`: output and source decoded through the
Kit's `LinearFrameDecoder` (Apple's pipeline) into linear Display P3 at
quarter scale; "vs source" is the worst channel of the whole-frame and
8×6-block means (blocks below 0.02 linear ignored — the noise floor, where
demosaics disagree by design). PSNR (peak 1.0 linear), SSIM (γ2.2 luma, 8×8
blocks) and an RMS error in stops (pixels above 0.5% of white) are measured
against the strategy's **lossless twin**, so a lossy row's numbers are the
codec's cost alone. The white-balance push decodes both files with the
balance moved *inside* the converter (`.cirawFilter`: ±2000 K and ±40 tint
declared at the as-shot neutral) and compares again.

- **Distance 0.5 (Adobe's default) is visually lossless on both routes**:
  57–61 dB and 0.07–0.14 stops RMS against the lossless twin, block means
  within 0.5% of it. Distance 1.0 costs ~4 dB and halves the file; 2.0
  another 3–4 dB for another third. Effort 3 costs 0.5–3 dB against effort
  7 for a third of the time; effort 5 is indistinguishable from 7 on Set 2.
- **Under a white-balance push the lossy files hold**: +2000 K / −2000 K /
  tint ±40 change the PSNR against the lossless twin by less than 1 dB on
  the dusk and dark frames (e.g. Set 1 d0.5: 61.1 → 61.1 / 59.8 / 60.3 dB).
  The night frame is the exception: the −2000 K push (which lifts blue,
  i.e. the noisiest channel) drops it to 50 dB on the demosaiced route and
  51 dB on the mosaic route — still far above visible, but the trend says
  low light plus a strong cool push is where distance 1.0 would start to
  show. Pushes on the Apple-decode sRGB files behave badly (Set 2: 22 / 15
  dB), one more reason for camera-native output.
- **The cubic (Adobe) carrier beats the gamma table at equal distance on
  file size** (Set 1: 0.83 vs 1.23 MB at d0.5, 59.6 vs 61.3 dB) because the
  table keeps a 3% pedestal for below-black noise that the cubic clips.
  Both are far above the visible threshold; the table keeps noise-floor
  fidelity (negatives survive), the cubic is smaller.
- **Chroma subsampling** is the 8-bit JPEG route's limit: ImageIO and the
  hardware encoder both write 4:2:0, which shows as block means 14–18% off
  on Set 2 (fine detail in colour) while the whole frame is within 2%.
- **Not measured**: Lightroom's rendering of our files (installed, not
  scriptable), long-sequence flicker of lossy frames (needs a blend of a
  converted sequence through `tools/flicker_report.py`), and anything on a
  device (§6).

## 4. Compatibility — what Apple's decoder does with each container shape

Measured with `CIRAWFilter(imageURL:)` (Preview, Photos and the app without
the repack), ImageIO (`CGImageSourceCreateImageAtIndex`), QuickLook
(`qlmanage -t`), the Kit (`LinearFrameDecoder`, which repacks Adobe-shaped
lossy containers) and Adobe DNG Converter (`-c` round trip, then its own
linear conversions of our file and of the input compared through Apple).

| shape | Apple direct | note |
|---|---|---|
| LinearRaw, **BlackLevel written once** for three samples | **wrong** — green ×1.6, blue ×2.2, red ×3.8 | writing it per sample renders within 0.5%. `DNGArchive` now always expands levels per sample. |
| LinearRaw + `LinearizationTable`, black > 0 (pedestal), LJ92 or JXL tiles | right | the gamma carrier of choice for LinearRaw |
| LinearRaw + `LinearizationTable`, **black 0**, JXL tiles | **black picture** | LJ92 with the same table and black 0 renders right; keep a pedestal |
| LinearRaw + `MapPolynomial` (cubic), black 0, LJ92 / JXL / 8-bit JPEG | right (means within 1.5%) | Adobe's shape minus the pedestal; the Kit repacks these (Adobe-shaped) files, the repack is exact |
| CFA + `LinearizationTable`, LJ92 | right | |
| CFA + `LinearizationTable`, **JXL** | **wrong** — table ignored, ×8 bright | |
| CFA + `MapPolynomial`, black 0, **JXL** | right (2–3%) | the lossy-mosaic shape |
| CFA + `MapPolynomial`, black 0, **LJ92** | **wrong** — polynomial ignored, ×8 bright | |
| CFA + JXL lossless (original profile, level-10 container) | right, bit-exact against LJ92 | 8% smaller than LJ92 |
| Adobe's own `-lossyMosaicJXL` (CFA + JXL + polynomial + black 1163) | **wrong** — red ×0.38 | the black-plus-opcode bug family of `LossyLinearDNG`, now on the mosaic; Adobe's file is 5.3 MB |
| 8-bit lossy JPEG (34892) + `LinearizationTable` | **black picture** (all level shapes tried) | |
| 8-bit lossy JPEG + `MapPolynomial`, black 0, white 255 | right (1.5%) | Adobe's legacy shape minus the pedestal; ImageIO and the hardware encoder both subsample chroma 4:2:0 |
| one tile for the whole frame (JXL 3873×2582, JPEG 3266×2449) | **wrong** at reduced scale (green ×0.2 / black) | and RawCamera crashed asynchronously after decoding the JXL one — keep tiles ≤ 512 |
| JXL LinearRaw 2304×1536 in 512×512 tiles | **RawCamera crash** (SIGSEGV, deterministic, any scale) | same pixels in 256×256 tiles, or as LJ92, decode fine; not characterised further |
| CFA DNG narrower than ~700 px | ImageIO sniffs `public.tiff`, no decode; `CIRAWFilter` opens it | only bites tests |
| BaselineExposure | applied (×4.06 for 2.0) to CFA and LinearRaw alike | Apple adds its **own** per-camera baseline (+0.35 EV for the ILCE-7M4) to CFA data by camera lookup, not to LinearRaw — a camera-native LinearRaw from a third-party raw must carry it explicitly; LibRaw has no such table, Adobe's DNG does |
| sRGB-declared LinearRaw (the Apple-decode path) | opens, renders 6% (Set 2) to 33% (ARW) darker than the source's own Apple decode, more with headroom | unexplained; the reason to prefer camera-native output |

Adobe DNG Converter re-read every container it was given (`-c` round trip
OK): our lossless CFA (LJ92 and JXL, bare or container) within 0.00–0.07%
of its decode of the ARW once BaselineExposure 0.35 is present (21.7%
without it — that is the missing baseline, not colour), and the lossy CFA
JXL within ~2%. Lightroom was not exercised (installed, not scriptable
here).

and `Sources/CLibRaw` module maps pointed at the framework headers — not done
in this spike, which links Homebrew on the Mac.

## 6. Recommendation

Three directions survive the matrix; the first is the one to build.

### 6.1 Camera-native LinearRaw JPEG XL — Adobe's file, made in the app

Kit parse of a LetsLapse DNG (or LibRaw unpack of a third-party raw) → Metal
Malvar-He-Cutler demosaic → Lanczos to the target size → gamma table with a
pedestal (or Adobe's cubic, black 0) → libjxl XYB, distance 0.5–1.0, effort
5, 512-px tiles → `DNGArchive` with per-sample levels, the source's colour
tags and BaselineExposure.

- **Numbers:** Set 2 8 MP: 1.28 MB at d0.5 (49 dB), 0.81 MB at d1.0 e5 (46 dB)
  in 0.46 s; Set 1 10 MP: 1.23 MB at d0.5 (61 dB) in ~1 s, 0.65 MB at d1.0.
  That is Adobe's size (1.3 MB / 0.7 MB) at Adobe's quality with a file that
  renders **directly** in Apple's decoder within 0.4% (Set 2) / 4% (Set 1,
  Apple's own Sony profile) of the source and re-decodes in Adobe within
  0.3–0.7%. Throughput on the Mac: 2.8–6 fps, 486 frames in 1.5–4 minutes.
- **Why not the mosaic:** lossy JPEG XL on the Bayer plane is competitive at
  dusk but drifts on the night frame and on clean blended frames (§2.2), and
  Apple only reads it through the MapPolynomial shape.
- **Open:** (1) ~~libjxl speed on the A18 / M3 iPad~~ measured (§2.5): 280 /
  185 ms per 8 MP frame, 0.5 / 0.4 s end to end; (2) BaselineExposure for third-party
  raws — Adobe's per-camera value is not in LibRaw or the DCP; candidates
  are a small table for the bodies we care about, or measuring it once per
  camera against Apple's own decode; (3) memory — Float16/UInt16
  intermediates to fit ~150 MB per frame on iPhone; (4) tile geometry —
  Apple's decoder crashed on one JXL layout (2304×1536, 512 tiles) and
  mis-renders whole-frame tiles; 512 tiles at 8–14 MP and 256 tiles below
  were clean, Adobe's 400×432 is the conservative choice; (5) the
  `LossyLinearDNG` repack now also triggers on our own cubic-carrier files
  (they are Adobe-shaped); harmless but 200 ms per decode — declining files
  with black 0 and no pedestal would skip it.

### 6.2 Keep the mosaic — lossless JPEG XL on the Bayer plane

Kit parse → the same CFA tiles through libjxl lossless (effort 7): **15.6 MB
in 0.09 s** against 17.1 MB for the Kit's lossless JPEG (0.10 s), and 12.8
vs 13.5 MB for the ARW's mosaic. Bit-exact, Apple and Adobe read it (the
tiles are level-10 container codestreams). A 9% win with no quality
question — the cheap first step for the capture pipeline itself
(`DNGAuthor.writeCompressedBayerDNG` could take a JXL encoder), not an
archive strategy on its own. Lossy on the mosaic (cubic MapPolynomial,
black 0) is worth keeping as an option for daylight material at d ≤ 0.5
(0.9–1.3 MB, 60 dB on the dusk/dark frames), with the night-frame caveat.

### 6.3 No third-party code — 8-bit lossy JPEG tiles with the cubic curve

Kit parse → Metal → cubic (Adobe's legacy shape, white 255, black 0) →
ImageIO JPEG q0.9 in 512 tiles: 1.4–1.5 MB in **0.14–0.34 s**, 44–54 dB,
Apple renders it directly, Adobe re-decodes it within 0.7–6%. Chroma is
4:2:0 (block means 14–18% off on fine colour) and 8 bits through a cubic
is JPEG-grade shadow precision — acceptable for a proxy tier, not the
archive. VideoToolbox's hardware JPEG brings nothing here: 0.36 s against
0.34 s for ImageIO, and its whole-frame tile renders black in Apple's DNG
reader.

**Not recommended:** the Apple-decode (sRGB LinearRaw) route — 6–33%
darker than Apple's own render of the source with a colour shift that grows
with BaselineExposure headroom, and white-balance pushes misbehave — and
VideoToolbox JPEG XL, which does not exist on any Apple platform measured.

### 6.4 Folding it into the app

Everything the app needs already compiles into the Kit: `LosslessJPEGDecoder`
(reads its own DNGs), the interleaved `LosslessJPEG` encoder, `DNGArchive`
(the DNG 1.4/1.7 tiled writer with the level rules baked in), the value
readers, `DNGCapabilityProbe`. The Metal demosaic/resizer, the curves and
the tile encoders live in `tools/dng-spike/Sources/dngspike` and are
iOS-clean Swift (Metal, MPS, vDSP, ImageIO) — they move into the Kit with a
test each. libjxl is the one dependency to add (BSD-3; 4.95 MB static
per slice before dead-stripping, §5), as a binary target from
`scripts/build-xcframeworks.sh`. LibRaw (CDDL) only matters for third-party
raws and can stay a Mac/import-time feature.

## 7. Traps recorded during the spike

- **Per-sample levels on LinearRaw.** Apple misapplies a single-value
  BlackLevel on a three-sample image (bright wash). Cost half a day.
- **Table vs polynomial is per codec in Apple's reader.** LJ92 CFA honours
  the LinearizationTable and ignores MapPolynomial; JXL CFA does the
  opposite; 8-bit lossy JPEG honours only the polynomial (black 0, white
  255); JXL LinearRaw honours the table only with a non-zero black.
- **Whole-frame tiles.** A single big tile renders wrong at reduced scale
  and one crashed RawCamera afterwards. 512 px tiles crashed RawCamera on a
  2304×1536 LinearRaw JXL; 256 px were fine.
- **RawCamera works asynchronously behind `CIRAWFilter`.** Creating a filter
  just to read `nativeSize` and dropping it, in a loop, crashed the bench
  inside RawCamera (SIGSEGV on a released buffer). Read sizes from the tags;
  keep a filter alive until its render is waited for.
- **libjxl picks codestream level 10 for 16-bit original-profile images** and
  wraps them in the ISOBMFF container even with `JxlEncoderUseContainer(false)`;
  Apple and Adobe both read the container tiles. XYB tiles stay bare.
- **Swift does not import C arrays past 4096 elements**: LibRaw's `cblack`
  (4102) needs a C shim.
- **LibRaw's `raw_inset_crops`** carries the camera's crop (4608×3072 inside
  4624×3080 for the A7 IV); apply it on even offsets to keep the CFA phase.
- **ImageIO JPEG at quality 0.9 subsamples chroma 4:2:0** (SOF sampling
  2×2,1×1,1×1), as does VideoToolbox's hardware JPEG — camera RGB chroma at
  half resolution.
- **`print` through a pipe is block-buffered**; a crash loses the log.
  `setlinebuf(stdout)`.
- **zsh does not word-split unquoted variables**; `set -- ${=var}`.
