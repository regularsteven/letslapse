All four chip-bearing adjust specs updated: [adjust.portrait.svg](adjust.portrait.svg) shows the burst variant (¼× ½× 1× 4× 15× ···, six wider chips), the reframe pair and [adjust.zoomed.portrait.svg](adjust.zoomed.portrait.svg) the base variant (1× 4× 15× 50× 60× 100× ···). Earlier the same day — **gap-aware eases: the camera's recording hole is absorbed kinematically**: even with straddling eases, Steven's re-render still jumped at the cuts. A four-agent forensic pass measured why: pixel-level train-motion analysis put the REAL inter-file hole at **~0.6s per boundary** (the sidecar's stamps bracket it from opposite sides — writer start-up latency vs stopwatch overshoot — and are each individually wrong), and frame-by-frame reads established the perceptual law: the no-ease hard cut reads fine because its big displacement sits among equally big displacements, while an ease that first slows to ~1× makes the same hole an 8–22× displacement outlier plus a luma blink. Fix: `SourceRegion.leadingGap` (midpoint of the sidecar brackets + 0.25s positive bias — overestimating reads as one calmer frame, underestimating as a glitch); an ease crossing a gapped boundary raises its split speed so the sweep crosses while per-frame displacement still exceeds the hole, and the two frames touching the cut keep the sweep's displacement while consuming only its footage share. `compile` was rewritten as **output-time integration** (each output frame = exactly 1/outFps of output time through the piece curve; sub-frame ease runs merge, region leftovers under half a frame are absorbed into the final frame), which the displacement-trace harness demanded — per-piece walks left runt frames at exactly the wrong places. A stretch eased at both ends now offers each seam half its budget (the first seam compiled can no longer starve the second). Result, measured on renders of the real shoot: worst displacement outlier at both cuts ~2× the local envelope (was 8–22×), schedules still frame-exact, and the honest cost — the burst funds the visible deceleration that bridges the hole — reported truthfully by the compiled captions (17s of ¼× → 8s with 1s eases). Previous sync 2026-08-10 (late evening) — **seam eases straddle their seams; warp axis rebuilt from the files**: Steven's Mac render of the "correct" sided config still showed a visible jump at the cut into the 100fps burst, and the frame-difference trace confirmed it — an isolated ~10× discontinuity spike at the seam. Structural cause: a side-owned ease plays entirely from one side's footage, and 25fps footage cannot render below 1× (no frames to show), so the planned 1×→¼× tail silently pinned at real time and the cut dropped playback 4× instantly; the sidecar drift then starved the ease's slowest frames at end-of-file, cutting from ~2–3× straight to ¼×. Fix, per Steven's own proposal: **the before/after side choice is GONE** (`Seam.side` removed from the model; old JSON decodes fine, the popover keeps only duration chips). Every ease now straddles its seam, split at the fast side's renderable floor — fast footage brakes through everything it can render and lands at the cut, the sub-floor tail (1×→¼×) plays from the burst's denser frames, and both sides render identical real-time cadence at the cut, so continuity is by construction. `easeSteps` 16→32 (the tail crosses the cut on sharp footage; run-to-run steps stay under ~35%). `compile` rewritten piece-exact: each piece's source frames counted once with a fractional carry and distributed evenly over its windows — schedules now match the files to the frame (5201/432/4666 on the test shoot), no drift, no runt windows at piece ends (plus a float-epsilon piece-advance fix and a pointer rewind at region starts, both of which the standalone harness caught as 1-frame cadence flashes at the cut). And the warp axis is now built from **probed file durations** (`CaptureProject.sourceSegmentSeconds`, filled by `refreshVideoMetadata`, preferred by `StretchBuilder.stretches`/`warpSourceRegions`/`warpFrameLocation`; stored timelines rebased per segment by `AppModel.healWarpAxis` on open) — the burst caption corrected 18s→17s on the real shoot and estimates equal renders. Harness verification: cadence step ratio at both cuts ≤1.33× (was 4.00×), eases apply in full (2s/2s), clamp warnings still fire when neighbours genuinely can't pay. Spec updates in [adjust.portrait.svg](adjust.portrait.svg) (pill labels lose their side arrows, popover loses the side chips, desc rewritten for straddle + piece-exact compile + probed axis). Previous sync same day (evening) — **warp ramps tell the truth**: a Mac test on a real ramp shoot (208s at 25fps · 4.5s burst at 100fps · 187s at 25fps) showed the seam eases silently self-destructing — an ease's source bill is duration × the mean of its eased speeds, both seams were sided onto the 4.5s burst, so the compiler ate 90% of the burst, collapsed the 2s ease to 0.16s and dropped the out-ease entirely, while the ramp-blind captions still read "18s of the clip" against an 8.9s total (1s and 2s ramps compiled identically — the clamp result is independent of the request). Four changes across `App/WarpTimeline.swift` + `App/WarpTimelineView.swift`: (1) eases interpolate in **log-speed** (equal ease time per halving, so a 50×→¼× warp reads as a steady deceleration instead of spending nine tenths of the ease looking fast — and costs ~19 rather than ~50 source seconds per 2s); (2) `compile` clips every blur window at its piece boundary, so a fast window no longer strides over the ease runs and the slow stretch behind them (this shoot's burst rendered 3 output frames before the fix, 66 after); (3) the compiled schedule reports per-seam `SeamEase` (requested vs applied) and per-stretch frame counts, a clamped or dropped ease **inverts its pill into a warning** — amber capsule, ink text, labelled with what actually compiles ("~0.2s ▶", "~0s ▶") — the popover adds an accent note naming the other side only when it genuinely has the budget, and picking a ramp duration with no side auto-sides the seam onto the stretch that can pay (`WarpTimeline.affordableSide`; both fit → the faster one); (4) the selection caption, tile widths and clip ruler all derive from the compiled per-stretch frames, so **the stretch captions always sum to the estimate card's total** (length÷speed survives only as the no-schedule fallback). ## Collections

The Collections feature (2026-08-01): an ordered set of blended clips from across projects on one timeline — per-clip in/out trims (fractions of the clip; trims retime the cut, clips always butt together), per-ratio crops (single-axis pan offset; collection-local override → clip default → centred), canvas ratios 16:9 · 9:16 · 1:1 · 4:3 · 3:4 exporting at 3840×2160 / 2160×3840 / 2160×2160 / 2880×2160 / 2160×2880, and a kept render that makes re-export instant while the recipe (clips + trims + crops + ratio + fps) is unchanged. Data model: `LapseCollection` in `App/CollectionsModel.swift`, persisted in `library.json`; default crops live on `BlendProject.defaultCrops`. One appearance per clip per collection; stills locked out (v1 video-only); deleting a blend or project drops it from every collection (the delete alert names them).
