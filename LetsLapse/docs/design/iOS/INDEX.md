Previous sync 2026-08-16 (third pass) — **focus becomes per-shoot, and pinch turns the right way round**. Field testing found focus set for one shoot could not be changed afterwards — not by tapping, not by starting a new shoot — with only a relaunch to clear it; pinching to change lens happened to free it, which is how it was found. Cause was ownership, not the lock itself: **nothing in the app ever handed the lens back to continuous auto-focus except three writes**, none at session start, and an `AVCaptureDevice` is process-global — its focus mode outlives the session, the `CameraController` and the screen, so a device left `.locked` came back locked with every tracking flag fresh and false, no hold to re-assert and no owner to release it. A lens change only freed it as a *side effect* (`refreshCaptureOptions` rewrites `activeFormat`, and `reassertFocusLock` declines to re-pin when nothing is held). Two more paths stranded it: `releaseRunFocusLock` and `lockFocusForRun` both bailed on `exposureLocked` — which the **brightness slider** latches — and a tap deliberately outlived its run with no control on screen to report or undo it. Separately, `applyTapFocus` could cancel the very hunt it asked for: 0.12s of grace was short enough that a lens leaving a hard lock had not begun moving, so the settle poll read the pre-hunt frame and `pinFocusHere` re-pinned the old plane — an accepted tap, a drawn reticle, nothing moved. Now: focus is a **per-shoot decision**. One `resumeContinuousAutoFocus` (which also re-centres `focusPointOfInterest`, per-device state that otherwise keeps weighting a stale tap) is the single hand-back, called at the end of every run, on every lens change, and once at `configureIfNeeded` so a stale device can never survive into a new screen. `focusIsHeld` drops `exposureLocked` for a truthful trio — `focusLocked` · the AE/AF lock's own `exposureLockHoldsFocus` · the focus slider's `manualLensHeld` — so brightness no longer freezes the lens while the focus slider now genuinely holds it. `lockedLensValue` became optional and **stamped with the camera that measured it** (positions are not comparable across constituents; the old `0.5` default was a plane nobody measured), falling back to "lock where you are". Tap and lens change now share one `captureIsRunning` truth, and `stop()` clears `intervalActive`/`isIntervalRunning` — a stranded published flag refused every tap for the life of the controller while pinch carried on working, the exact asymmetry that made pinching look like a fix. And the pinch itself was inverted: fingers **apart** now step tighter (1× → 3×), together step wider, matching native Camera and the app's own photo-grid pinch. Previous sync 2026-08-16 — **focus is locked for the whole of a shoot, and tappable before one**: a shot composed in focus was being pulled out of focus by the act of recording it. `applyCaptureFormat` assigns `activeFormat`, which hands the lens back to continuous auto-focus, and `startRecording` pins the run to one physical lens (a device-input swap) that does the same — so the shutter press re-opened a hunt over the opening frames, and every burst boundary re-opened one mid-shoot, where the only thing that may change is the frame rate. The re-assert existed but lived inside `reassertExposureLock`, which returns early unless the user had manually locked *exposure*; focus is now its own `reassertFocusLock`, gated on a new `focusIsHeld` (tap · either lock button · the run's own hold) and called unconditionally from both format paths, with a no-op guard so a fast-path burst boundary never writes to a lens already where it should be. Every run — video, interval, Photo, live blend and its DNG twin — now takes `lockFocusForRun` after any input swap and before its first frame, pinning the lens exactly where the user left it (`awaitFocusSettle` first, so a lens caught mid-hunt is never frozen between planes), and `releaseRunFocusLock` hands it back at the end. An unlock pressed mid-shoot returns exposure and white balance but **not** the lens. A tap outlives the run that used it, so a second take lands on the same plane. Previous sync 2026-08-13 — **crash-safe capture session logs**: a shoot that dies with the camera open used to leave nothing behind to look at. `App/CaptureSessionLogger.swift` now writes one newline-delimited JSON event per line to `Application Support/LetsLapse/CaptureLogs/capture-<UUID>.log` (`FileHandle` seekToEnd + write on a serial queue, so a crash can only ever truncate the last line) and **deletes the file when the session ends normally** — which makes a surviving log, by construction, a crash or a force-quit. `CameraController` feeds it: session_start (device · iOS · build · mode · initial format), format_change from `publishFormat` (deduped — it re-fires on every burst segment switch), burst_set, burst_start/burst_end with sequence-relative offsets, exposure_lock/unlock with the ISO/shutter/lens the lock froze at, capture_start/capture_end per run, and stop_requested tagged with who asked (phone · watch · scheduled · rig); `WatchRemoteControlReceiver` logs every watch_command including the ones the phone's guards refuse. The one non-obvious wiring: `CaptureView.cleanUpOnDisappear()` does **not** stop the camera (only the X button and the finish handlers do, and Photo mode keeps it live on purpose), so the log is closed from both ends or every non-X exit would leave a false orphan. Earlier the same day — **gap-aware eases: the camera's recording hole is absorbed kinematically**: even with straddling eases, Steven's re-render still jumped at the cuts. A four-agent forensic pass measured why: pixel-level train-motion analysis put the REAL inter-file hole at **~0.6s per boundary** (the sidecar's stamps bracket it from opposite sides — writer start-up latency vs stopwatch overshoot — and are each individually wrong), and frame-by-frame reads established the perceptual law: the no-ease hard cut reads fine because its big displacement sits among equally big displacements, while an ease that first slows to ~1× makes the same hole an 8–22× displacement outlier plus a luma blink. Fix: `SourceRegion.leadingGap` (midpoint of the sidecar brackets + 0.25s positive bias — overestimating reads as one calmer frame, underestimating as a glitch); an ease crossing a gapped boundary raises its split speed so the sweep crosses while per-frame displacement still exceeds the hole, and the two frames touching the cut keep the sweep's displacement while consuming only its footage share. `compile` was rewritten as **output-time integration** (each output frame = exactly 1/outFps of output time through the piece curve; sub-frame ease runs merge, region leftovers under half a frame are absorbed into the final frame), which the displacement-trace harness demanded — per-piece walks left runt frames at exactly the wrong places. A stretch eased at both ends now offers each seam half its budget (the first seam compiled can no longer starve the second). Result, measured on renders of the real shoot: worst displacement outlier at both cuts ~2× the local envelope (was 8–22×), schedules still frame-exact, and the honest cost — the burst funds the visible deceleration that bridges the hole — reported truthfully by the compiled captions (17s of ¼× → 8s with 1s eases). Previous sync 2026-08-10 (late evening) — **seam eases straddle their seams; warp axis rebuilt from the files**: Steven's Mac render of the "correct" sided config still showed a visible jump at the cut into the 100fps burst, and the frame-difference trace confirmed it — an isolated ~10× discontinuity spike at the seam. Structural cause: a side-owned ease plays entirely from one side's footage, and 25fps footage cannot render below 1× (no frames to show), so the planned 1×→¼× tail silently pinned at real time and the cut dropped playback 4× instantly; the sidecar drift then starved the ease's slowest frames at end-of-file, cutting from ~2–3× straight to ¼×. Fix, per Steven's own proposal: **the before/after side choice is GONE** (`Seam.side` removed from the model; old JSON decodes fine, the popover keeps only duration chips). Every ease now straddles its seam, split at the fast side's renderable floor — fast footage brakes through everything it can render and lands at the cut, the sub-floor tail (1×→¼×) plays from the burst's denser frames, and both sides render identical real-time cadence at the cut, so continuity is by construction. `easeSteps` 16→32 (the tail crosses the cut on sharp footage; run-to-run steps stay under ~35%). `compile` rewritten piece-exact: each piece's source frames counted once with a fractional carry and distributed evenly over its windows — schedules now match the files to the frame (5201/432/4666 on the test shoot), no drift, no runt windows at piece ends (plus a float-epsilon piece-advance fix and a pointer rewind at region starts, both of which the standalone harness caught as 1-frame cadence flashes at the cut). And the warp axis is now built from **probed file durations** (`CaptureProject.sourceSegmentSeconds`, filled by `refreshVideoMetadata`, preferred by `StretchBuilder.stretches`/`warpSourceRegions`/`warpFrameLocation`; stored timelines rebased per segment by `AppModel.healWarpAxis` on open) — the burst caption corrected 18s→17s on the real shoot and estimates equal renders. Harness verification: cadence step ratio at both cuts ≤1.33× (was 4.00×), eases apply in full (2s/2s), clamp warnings still fire when neighbours genuinely can't pay. Previous sync same day (evening) — **warp ramps tell the truth**: a Mac test on a real ramp shoot (208s at 25fps · 4.5s burst at 100fps · 187s at 25fps) showed the seam eases silently self-destructing — an ease's source bill is duration × the mean of its eased speeds, both seams were sided onto the 4.5s burst, so the compiler ate 90% of the burst, collapsed the 2s ease to 0.16s and dropped the out-ease entirely, while the ramp-blind captions still read "18s of the clip" against an 8.9s total (1s and 2s ramps compiled identically — the clamp result is independent of the request). Four changes across `App/WarpTimeline.swift` + `App/WarpTimelineView.swift`: (1) eases interpolate in **log-speed** (equal ease time per halving, so a 50×→¼× warp reads as a steady deceleration instead of spending nine tenths of the ease looking fast — and costs ~19 rather than ~50 source seconds per 2s); (2) `compile` clips every blur window at its piece boundary, so a fast window no longer strides over the ease runs and the slow stretch behind them (this shoot's burst rendered 3 output frames before the fix, 66 after); (3) the compiled schedule reports per-seam `SeamEase` (requested vs applied) and per-stretch frame counts, a clamped or dropped ease **inverts its pill into a warning** — amber capsule, ink text, labelled with what actually compiles ("~0.2s ▶", "~0s ▶") — the popover adds an accent note naming the other side only when it genuinely has the budget, and picking a ramp duration with no side auto-sides the seam onto the stretch that can pay (`WarpTimeline.affordableSide`; both fit → the faster one); (4) the selection caption, tile widths and clip ruler all derive from the compiled per-stretch frames, so **the stretch captions always sum to the estimate card's total** (length÷speed survives only as the no-schedule fallback).
